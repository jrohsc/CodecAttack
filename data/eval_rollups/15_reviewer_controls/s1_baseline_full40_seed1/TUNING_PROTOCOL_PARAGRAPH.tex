We evaluate the S1 controls against Qwen2-Audio-7B-Instruct
(snapshot \texttt{0a095220c30b7b31434169c3086508ef3ea5bf0a}).
The 50 carrier--target pairs are split by source recording into 10 development
and 40 held-out pairs. Four development pairs select the waveform step-size
ratio from $\alpha/\epsilon\in\{0.02,0.05,0.1,0.2\}$ by maximizing exact
complete-target substring matches across clean delivery and Opus
$\{16,24,32,64,128\}$\,kbps, breaking ties by clean matches and then the
smaller ratio; both tuned waveform arms select $\alpha/\epsilon=0.02$.
The selected pilot attacks respect the latent reference's distortion ceiling,
although the candidate ratios were not all matched to the same SNR.
Relative to the original waveform baseline (fixed $\epsilon=0.06082$,
$\alpha/\epsilon=0.2$, 20\% clean warmup, Opus updates at every subsequent
step, and its original carrier normalization), the tuned unrestricted arm
uses the common peak-normalized 24\,kHz carrier, a 30\% clean warmup followed
by alternating clean and Opus-BPDA updates, and a per-example SNR-calibrated
waveform budget. The $\leq$4\,kHz arm uses the same recipe with an exact
frequency-bin projection of the \emph{additive perturbation}; ``unrestricted''
means that the tuned arm omits this 4\,kHz mask, not that it replaces the
carrier. The tuned arm directly optimizes bounded 16\,kHz waveform samples
against the victim's target-text loss, with no perceptual-loss term; it
does not append spoken target audio or alter neural-codec latents.
Accordingly, matched SNR does not establish that this control remains
benign-sounding. Both tuned arms use Adam for 1,000 steps, one zero-initialized run
per tested budget (no random restarts), and the last iterate, with no
attack-success-based checkpoint selection.
Held-out budgets use only carrier-referenced SNR on saved and reloaded
24\,kHz PCM waveforms: each tuned arm required 40 distortion probes and 40
initial calibrated runs; the unrestricted arm required two further
correction runs and the $\leq$4\,kHz arm required 16, 8, 5, and 3 across
successive correction rounds (82 and 112 held-out optimizations in total).
The final $\leq$4\,kHz repairs added 55 minutes 11 seconds of allocated GPU
time, and both tuned arms satisfy the $\pm1$\,dB gate on all 40 pairs.
No held-out ASR output selected a learning rate, budget, checkpoint, or
calibration procedure. CodecAttack and the original waveform arm reuse their
historical artifacts; CodecAttack may restore a periodically successful
checkpoint, whereas the waveform arms save the last iterate. Consequently,
the four-arm comparison reports outcomes under these documented recipes and
does not isolate perturbation parameterization alone.
